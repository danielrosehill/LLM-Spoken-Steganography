Allotment Garden Reopens Its Gates

Maya: Anyone who has walked past Larch Street lately has noticed the gates are open again.
Owen: Absolutely, the allotment society reopened the site on Saturday after a long winter closure.
Maya: Crowds turned up with spades, seed trays and a surprising number of thermoses.
Owen: Deborah Hale, who has run plot nine for years, said the soil was still cold.
Maya: Apparently the shared water tank had frozen solid over January, so volunteers spent the morning thawing it.
Owen: Coffee and biscuits were on offer in the old shed.
Maya: Well, the society is asking newcomers to sign up for a plot before the end of March.
Owen: Fees are modest, and there is a waiting list for the larger beds near the hedge.
Maya: Gardeners who want to help with the compost bins can just turn up on Thursday evenings.
Owen: Families are especially welcome, and the society has put up a small play corner by the gate.
Maya: Honestly, the best part is the bench someone carved from an old apple tree.
Owen: Hannah from the parish council says the next open day is in May.
Maya: All the details will be posted on the noticeboard outside the library.
Owen: Dates and times for the seed swap are still being confirmed, so keep an eye out.
Maya: If you cannot make it on Saturday, the society says a few plots will stay open until Sunday.
Owen: Cuttings and bulbs are also being given away free to anyone who asks.
Maya: Nobody needs a big garden to take part, just a bit of patience.
Owen: Officially the site has reopened after a long refurbishment of the water system.
Maya: Careful pruning of the old raspberry canes is on the list for next week.
Owen: Walking tours of the site are planned for later in the summer.
Maya: Once the tours are confirmed, the society will post them online too.
Owen: Winter squash, runner beans and a few stubborn leeks are already growing well.
Maya: Whoever planted those leeks last autumn deserves a medal for patience.
Owen: People keep saying the first sunny weekend always brings the best turnout.
Maya: Children from the primary school planted sunflowers along the fence on Friday.
Owen: Nearly every bed has a new label now, written by the kids in bright marker pens.
Maya: Sadly the old scarecrow blew over in Tuesday's storm and needs rebuilding.
Owen: The committee has already agreed to give it a new hat.
Maya: Blackberry bushes along the eastern wall are finally getting trimmed back.
Owen: Dozens of volunteers turned up with loppers and gloves for the job.
Maya: Pretty much everyone agreed the morning ended with cake and a lot of mud.
Owen: Let us hope the weather holds for the next few weeks.
Maya: Odd as it sounds, the rain has been the biggest help so far.
Owen: Soon the wildflower strip by the car park should be in full bloom.
Maya: Plenty of bees have already moved in, which the society is thrilled about.
Owen: Locals can find the full timetable on the society's website this week.
Maya: Meanwhile the café by the station has promised to keep a pot of tea going all day.
Owen: Good news for anyone who likes a slice of cake after digging.
Maya: So that is the update from the allotments, and it sounds like a lovely start.
Owen: Cheers to the volunteers who made it happen, and see you down there.
Maya: Come along on Saturday and bring a pair of wellies.
Owen: Thanks for tuning in to the local news today.
Maya: Enjoy the sunshine, and mind the mud on the paths.
